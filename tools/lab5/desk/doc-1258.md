# Adversarial Audit #1 — performance dimension only

**Date:** 2026-09-04 · **Auditor:** Otto (red-teaming own plan per Mark's order) · **Scope:** strategy + architecture as performance machine. Legal/regulatory out of scope per Mark.

---

## A. Bad — and fixable (cards filed)

**A1. The "milliseconds" defense claim is currently false.** desk_cron runs on a `*/15` cron. If invalidation exits are evaluated there, a stop is "within 15 minutes," not milliseconds — BTC can move 5–10% in that window; at 3x that's 15–30% of a sleeve position. **Fix:** invalidation exits live as **venue-native trigger orders** (HL supports TP/SL triggers on-venue — they fire even if NewDev is down); desk_cron defense becomes backup, plus a fast (30–60s) daemon for anything venues can't hold natively. Kill word gets a fast path too. *Card: Infrastructure, high.*

**A2. The confluence gate is unfalsifiable as written.** "Structure + flows + calendar + invalidation" with no numeric thresholds = vibes wearing a rubric. The weekly review can't score it, the journal can't falsify it, and "gate passed" means whatever the woken session feels like. **Fix:** Phase 0 defines numbers — funding APR floor, MA structure conditions, momentum bounds, event window, invalidation = explicit price. *Card: Strategy & Docs, high.*

**A3. The LLM's discretionary value is an unpriced hypothesis.** If the gate is fully quantified (A2), a pure rules system could enter too — so what is the agent for? Honest answer: novelty handling and catalyst judgment. But that's a claim, not a fact. **Fix:** paper phase runs a **shadow mechanical baseline** alongside the agent's decisions; we measure whether discretion adds or destroys return before live. *Card: Paper Trading, high.*

**A4. Zero historical contact.** The rules were written from one episode (Aug–Sep 2026: the $80–82k rejection, the Waller pump). That's recency bias baked into the foundation. **Fix:** cheap replay of the quantified gate over 2024–2026 BTC/ETH before paper — not full backtest infra, just "does this rubric do anything sane on known history." *Card: Infrastructure, normal.*

**A5. Wake-lock suppresses by fingerprint, not by magnitude.** A market grinding through three levels of the same trigger type can be suppressed as "same fingerprint" while the state has materially changed. **Fix:** wake on state-delta (price/funding moved > threshold since last wake), not only on new trigger types. *Card: Infrastructure, normal.*

**A6. Degraded mode is unspecified.** If multihost (control plane) is unreachable: does NewDev fail open (positions without defense config) or fail closed (flatten on an infra blip)? Both are bad in different directions. **Fix:** spec it — control plane down → hold, rely on venue-native stops, alert Mark; never auto-flatten on infra failure. *Folded into A5 card.*

**A7. Event-risk rule covers entries, not existing positions.** "No new leveraged positions within 12h of FOMC/CPI" — but does the sleeve *hold* leverage through the print? Undefined. **Fix:** explicit policy (reduce/hold-with-wider-invalidation) as a risk-rule amendment. *Card: Strategy & Docs, normal.*

**A8. Friction is a top-3 drag at $1k and the doc doesn't budget it.** Basis round-trip ≈ 4+ transactions; weekly calendar rebalancing bleeds fees on a $650 engine; bridge hops cost 1–2% of the purse one-time. **Fix:** maker/post-only orders where possible, rebalance on 5pp drift bands not calendar, one L2, bridge once. *Comment on playbooks card #3354.*

**A9. Corrupt-packet risk.** The woken agent decides from the TradePacket; a packet bug = deciding on wrong numbers. **Fix:** packet carries raw values + invariants; agent verifies before acting; Phase 0 drills include a corrupt-packet refusal drill. *Comment on drills card #3356.*

## B. Bad — structural / not yet considered (bound, don't fix)

**B1. The harvester is in a race it mostly loses.** Liquidations are a speed game; dedicated bots win in one block. A cron-cadence harvester wins ~nothing; even event-driven scanning only finds the long tail (weird collateral, tiny tickets) where competition is thin. The design's instinct is right but expectations must be: **rare wins.** The $5/day revert-gas cap makes finding out cheap. Scanner must be per-block/event-driven to have any hit rate at all.

**B2. The barbell's three legs can lose simultaneously in a flash crash.** Sleeve longs get stopped (slippage in a panic), funding flips (engine exits into a gapped basis — "market neutral" ≠ "crash neutral": spot-perp basis dislocates exactly when everyone exits), and HL can **ADL** the engine's profitable short leg, force-breaking delta neutrality at the worst moment. The 20% DD cap is the backstop; nothing else protects this.

**B3. Stack counterparty risk is unpriced.** USDC depeg, AAVE exploit, HL bridge/oracle failure, L2 sequencer failure — the purse can be impaired with zero bad trades. At $1k the EV of mitigation is low, but venue/asset diversification is nearly free where available.

**B4. Model drift.** The decision plane's behavior changes across model versions; the same packet can get different decisions next quarter. Bound by: quantified gate (A2), journaling model version per decision, weekly review watching for decision-quality shifts.

**B5. Engine dollar-yield vs. complexity at this size.** Engine earns ~$8/month on $650. Its real job is ballast + proof-of-mechanic, not income. If the purse's purpose were pure growth, barbell weights would be debatable — but the ballast is what makes sleeve aggression survivable, and weights are Mark's to set.

## Verdict

Nothing fatal. The worst finding (A1) is an architecture gap with a clean venue-native fix. The most important finding (A3) is that the desk's core premise — agent discretion adds value over rules — must be *measured* in paper, not assumed. Fix A1–A9 in Phase 0; bound B1–B5 with caps and honest expectations.
